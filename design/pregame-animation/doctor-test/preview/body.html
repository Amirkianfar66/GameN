The new world

  • The crew, redefined: the same faces, hair and suits, drawn again in a heavier ink. A swelling silhouette line, hatching where the light falls away, worn cloth, and gear that has seen use: a harness with a work-light, a belt pouch, repair tape on the forearm.
  • The briefing room is a ready room on the dock's service deck. Bolted bulkhead plates, pipes with a valve and a gauge, a rag tied round a leaking joint, sagging cables, a ribbed duct with a turning fan, a wall terminal and a workbench of tools. Its light is cold steel, with two warm accents: the desk lamp on her hand and face, and the terminal's cyan.
  • Through the port is the cosmic view: a ringed giant, a swirling nebula in teal, magenta and gold, orbits with moons running on them, the docked ship's lights, and a tug crossing.
  • Colony Nine is rust and ochre. A patched prefab ward, a dripping pipe, a fan, the dust storm wheeling in the porthole, a monitor's green trace, and the lamp swinging its light across the cots.
  • The cut at 3,000 ms is a cosmic event. Orbital rings open from the glint and rush outward, changing scale. Light swirls, and it all folds back into the ward's lamp.

The acting

  • 0–4,000 ms: the only stretch where the character acts at a readable size.
  • Key poses and breakdowns are posed on the approved rig. The in-betweens follow a timing chart, on twos (a new drawing every 1/12 s). The camera, light, smoke and machinery move on every screen frame.
  • The actions: a breath and a sink back before the reach, a stretch on the reach, a settle on the grip, an arc on the lift and an overshoot at the face. The hair follows through. The eyes lead the head. A slow blink marks the decision.
  • No words on screen and no sound. Reduce motion shows two held pictures, each faded in gently.
