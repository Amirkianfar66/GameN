**How often a random policy reaches an outcome is not recorded, on purpose.** It would look like a win rate and would mean nothing: random players do not persuade, bluff or vote like people.

## 9. Corrections

Each is listed because a changed expectation is the easiest way to manufacture a pass. **None of them changed an expectation to agree with the engine.** The engine passed every ready case of the first revision and passes every ready case of this one.

### After the integration review

The review assigned two findings to Balance and asked for the register and the contract review to be reconciled with the approved decisions and the newer documents.

1. **R1: consent was not checked on incomplete playtest records.** The record validator ran its per-participant checks only for a complete record. An incomplete record that held a participant with consent false, with no consent field, or with an identifier that is not a pseudonym was reported as valid; incomplete records are the normal case until the server export exists. Now every participant of any record that is not a blank template needs a pseudonym and recorded consent, a template may hold no participant, and a record's shape is checked whatever its status. The review's probe now reports the missing consent, and its second probe, a template with no sections, reports six missing sections. Regressions: an incomplete record with consent false, absent or not a boolean; an invalid, missing or repeated pseudonym; a template carrying a participant.
2. **R2: the controls command succeeded although its baselines failed.** A ready scenario that failed unmodified was counted and skipped, and the command exited 0 unless an executed control was missed. Now a run passes only when every baseline passed, at least one control ran and every control was detected; otherwise the command prints each failing baseline and exits 1. A missing engine is reported apart: NOT RUN and exit 0, or exit 2 with `--require-engine`, on all three engine commands. Regressions start the real commands against a stand-in engine that refuses every setup and assert exit status 1, and against a missing engine and assert 0 and 2. On the real draft engine the reworked command reproduces the earlier counts exactly.
3. **Two more defects of the same kind, found while reproducing R2.** With an engine that refuses every setup, a blocked case was reported as failed, although the runner's own claim is that a blocked case stays blocked whatever an engine does; and the playout command crashed with a stack trace instead of reporting the playout as unfinished. Both are fixed and have regressions.
4. **The register was triaged against V1-01 to V1-21.** Nothing approved is asked again. D13 and D29 are resolved by V1-04 and V1-10. D15 is answered by the existing five-round and showdown structure, so the blocked case that waited on it is withdrawn: one fixture fewer in each mode. D10 is outside Version 1. The sixteen remaining readings ask for no approval. The nine open rule edges are on one sheet. The rulebook is at revision 3; the audit lists what changed.
5. **The contract review was re-read against the newer Backend and Frontend documents.** The finding on `shotAvailable` is closed, the request for one combined ruleset pin is withdrawn, and the review states Balance's position on Backend's four proposed refinements. The request that the owner confirm the approval of V1-01 to V1-21 once more is withdrawn.

### After the independent checks

Before review was requested, the rulebook and fixtures were checked three times by separate Claude Code agents that were given the files and not the author's conclusions. None of them is a human review.

- **The rulebook against its sources.** Every one of the 155 rows of revision 1 was read against the sources it cites. It found one row that contradicted two others, an unqualified "may not shoot", a case the text left out, two questions registered as open that the sources answer, statuses that claimed more than their source, and corners with no row.
- **Every ready eight-player scenario, derived again by hand.** All 129 ready eight-player scenarios of revision 1 were derived from the rulebook alone, without the engine. No wrong expected result and no wrong arithmetic was found. It found order-of-resolution cases written in the order the commands were sent, expectations that no rule implies, and citations that were too weak.
- **The added and changed scenarios, in all three modes.** After those corrections, 22 scenario codes were derived again by hand in each mode: 66 fixtures. Again no wrong result. 51 were fully supported, and 15 rested on something they did not name: a working reading used without being named, one check that no rule row stated, and one outcome that needed a rule the case did not cite.

The corrections that followed the third check were not derived a fourth time by an independent reader. That covers the two rows it led to (R-FLOW-13 and R-VOTE-10), the new case `REL-07`, and the count of elections restored in `CAPT-10`.

What changed as a result:

1. **The rulebook went to revision 2.** The audit lists every row that changed. Two questions that were registered as open, D13 and D14, turned out to be answered by the sources when read closely. Their cases `CAPT-09` and `WIN-06` went from blocked probes to ready scenarios, and both pass.
2. **The order-of-resolution cases were rebuilt in turn order.** `ORDER-01`, `ORDER-03`, `ORDER-04a`, `ORDER-04b` and `SHOW-04` are about effects that resolve in turn order. Their first versions were written in the order the commands were sent, so in some modes the attacked actor's own action resolved before the attack on it and the case passed without exercising its rule. Each now reads the recorded turn order: the attacked actor is the one whose turn comes last, and `ORDER-04b` uses a seed that reverses the two attackers of `ORDER-04a`.
3. **Expectations that no rule row stated were removed, or given a row.** Removed: a ballot that abstains explicitly (`FLOW-03`, `CAPT-03`; the rules know only the missing ballot), the moment at which a release request counts as used (`REL-01`), and a window that closes late (`FLOW-02`). Given a row: that no window opens which nobody could use (R-FLOW-13, reading D37; `CAPT-10`, `REL-02`, `REL-05`, `REL-06`, `REL-07`), and that a release takes effect when its vote closes (R-VOTE-10, reading D38; `REL-01`, `REL-04`, `REL-06`). The release cases no longer close the choice window themselves: whether that window closes at once is open rule edge D17.
4. **One case was withdrawn.** It expected a shot command carrying an identification, a faction guess or a damage value to be refused. That is a property of the wire contract, not a rule. It is recorded in the contract review instead.
5. **R-RES-03 has a case that does not depend on D14.** The earlier attempt ended in a state that D14 decides and was blocked. `SHOW-10` now eliminates every Red player and then injures every Healthy Blue player within one showdown, so that a check between two shots would find a Blue win and the check after the stage finds none. The earlier content is `SHOW-16`, ready under reading D14.
6. **Titles that claimed more than their steps show were corrected**, and one step that showed nothing was dropped: `PROT-05`, `SUP-01`, `ORDER-05`, `RESC-01`, and `WIN-09`, which was split so that `WIN-10` shows that an Injured Alien adds no Power.
7. **Every scenario names the further rules it uses**, in `dependsOn`, so that the scenarios touching a reading can be found. The third check found readings used without being named, such as the Captain's way out of Command Room (D33) in five cases, and one outcome that needed R-ACT-05 without citing it (`WIN-06`). The names are now read mechanically from each scenario's steps. New cases cover the readings added in revision 2 (`CAPT-11` to `CAPT-13`, `SCAN-07`), the public phase facts (`VIEW-03`) and a released player voted back into Jail (`REL-06`).
8. **Two invariants were narrowed and one was added.** INV-PH-02 and INV-VIEW-03 do not judge the Captain's own release choice closing its window, which is open (D17). INV-VIEW-07 is silent about an aborted match, which is open (D35). INV-PH-07 is new: no window opens that nobody could use (reading D37). It held in every scenario and playout.

### While producing the first evidence

1. **An invariant was over-broad.** The first run failed six scenarios on INV-EL-03, which then said that an Eliminated player may never hold a special shot. A participant eliminated during the showdown keeps an unused shot flag in server state after the match. No rule forbids that: R-SHOW-02 grants shots when the showdown opens. The invariant now checks what the rule says, that nobody who was Eliminated before the showdown holds one.
2. **One scenario was scripted wrongly.** In the nine-player `SHOW-14` a player already Eliminated was listed as a voter. The engine refused the ballot, correctly. The voter list was fixed.
3. **The rulebook misstated Scan.** A draft of R-ROLE-13 read as if Hacker could Scan Undercover from another room. The baseline requires the same location for every other player. The rule was reworded and `SCAN-03` now covers it.
4. **The adapter did not report a pending Protection.** The engine holds a grant as a queued command until its round resolves. The binding now reports that as pending. This is a mapping, not an expectation.
5. **The audit's map of owner decisions missed one rule.** `npm run check` caught that R-FLOW-11 cites V1-10 and was not listed. Fixed.
6. **One control was not a real change.** Reversing an empty list of expected turns changes nothing. The control now appends a seat instead.
