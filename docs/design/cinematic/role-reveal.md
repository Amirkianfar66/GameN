# Role reveal films: one ten-second film per role (proposal)

Designer proposal, 6 November 2026. The Doctor's pilot: branch `claude/designer-role-reveal`, stacked
on #184. The other films: branch `claude/designer-role-films`, stacked on #187.

The owner asked for a short film after each player sees their role, one per role, of five to ten
seconds:

> می خوام بازیکن ها بعد از دیدن نقش شون یک animation کوتاه ببین پس کلا به ازای هر role یک animation 5 to 10 ثانیه ای . نظرت برای هر Role چیه

("I want players to see a short animation after seeing their role, so one animation of 5 to 10
seconds for each role. What is your idea for each role?")

The owner approved the Designer's plan: a fixed length for every role, the shared rules below, and
one pilot (the Doctor) before the other eight. The owner then set the creative brief:

> یا کل طرح و ایده ات موافقم فقط دوست دارم ازتباط انیمشن هر نقش با نقش و کارایی اش و فضای space و sci fi بازی و روح نوآر کمیک بوک هماهگ باشه و به داستان بتزی و محتوا بخوره

("I agree with the whole plan and idea. I only want each role's animation to match the role and
what it does, the game's space and sci-fi setting and the noir comic-book spirit, and to fit the
game's story and content.")

The owner reviewed the seven-second pilot and asked for a first part:

> ویدیو دکتر ۷ ثانیه ایت خیلی خوبه برای توضیح نقش اما بنظرم یک تیکه اول می خوایم که نشون بده یارو دکتر شده شاید مانند اینکه character قبل سوار شدن به spaceship , gadget hospital  رو‌بر‌ میداره  از روی یک میز که روی میز کارت Doctor هست بعد اش این ۳ پنل رو‌ببینه که ساختی

("Your seven-second Doctor video is very good for explaining the role, but I think we want a first
part that shows the guy has become the Doctor: maybe like the character, before boarding the
spaceship, picks up the Hospital gadget from a table with the Doctor card on it, and after that sees
these three panels you made.")

Every film now opens in the briefing room at the dock, where the player's own character takes the
role's gadget from the desk where their role card stands (sections 2 and 4).

After the nine-second pilot, the owner asked for a story, and then said what kind:

> I like it much better but can we have a little bit storyline feeling

> sory to intrupt you but story can be little back story maybe it is not really the story of the game in spaceship imagine it like a comic =book each character has a back story

The three panels now tell a small back story, as a comic book gives its characters one. The first
two are memory, in sepia: who the role-holder was before the voyage. The third comes aboard, to
what the role does. A narration box rides each panel. Every film is now ten seconds: inside the
owner's five to ten, and the same for every role.

The owner liked the back story and asked for more finish:

> I like it just can we make it better quality like animated comicbook

The films are now finished as a motion comic. A memory flash leads out of the gadget's glint, and a
guided camera moves panel by panel and pulls back to the page. The narration is set in comic
capitals and drops into place. Figures are inked with bold outlines, the panel edges are
cross-hatched, and the whole page is printed on paper grain. The figures breathe, look and sway.
The review renders are twice as sharp (section 2, "The look").

The owner then asked for fewer words:

> Lets reduce texts in animation it is like tutorial

The back story is now told mostly in pictures. Only the two memory panels keep a narration box,
of a few words each. The panel aboard and the title carry no line, and the rules stay on the role
card. Each box is lettered as a comic's caption, a sentence to a line ("COLONY NINE." over "THE
FEVER YEARS."), and fitted to its words.

The owner then approved the fewer-words pilot:

> Lets use this one

The other films are now drawn from it, each with its own back story (section 3), in the same
briefing room, clock, palette and look; the two Disablers share one film. Every panel aboard is
wordless but for a sound word: the stamped words the first storyboards proposed (label tapes, a
ROUND 3 stamp, CAPTURED, UNLOCKED) are left to the role cards.

This page holds the storyboards for all nine roles and the pilot's specification. Nothing here is
a rule. A film plays only inside the existing role-reading stage and takes no time from it.

## 1. The shared rules: what makes a reveal private

A player's phone sits on a table among other players. A role film must tell its own player everything
and a neighbour nothing.

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Every film lasts exactly 10,000 ms**, full and reduced, and its beats change at the same moments: 3,000, 4,500, 6,000, 7,800 and 9,300 ms. The opener's moves (the reach, the take, the raise) keep the same times in every film, and every film has the same two panels of memory and one aboard | A neighbour who sees a phone's screen change, or times it, learns nothing |
| **Silent, with no vibration** | The art direction: "Phones produce no role-specific or secret-action sound" |
| **One palette and one brightness for every role:** ink, paper, steel and amber focus. A team's colour appears only small: in the emblem on the title plate, and on the role card standing on the desk (its device picture and its team swatch, as on the phone's card). The gadget the character raises is drawn large, so its team accent is taken out and it catches the room's starlight, the same for every role | A big red or blue flash would be visible across the table. A test holds every colour drawn, the gadget's included, at least 30 (CIE76) from each faction accent; the card's swatch is the one exception, and the test checks it is the dealt role's own. Each beat's brightness is compared across roles once a second film exists |
| **Own phone only**, during the role-reading stage (the 30-second minimum read). A tap skips to the card. It never replays on a reconnect or a refresh. The card may offer "Watch again" | The reading stage is private, and a film is never an event anyone else gets |
| **No other player appears.** The opener and the back story draw the player's own character: their lobby character, public, and the same whatever the role. It is the only figure in colour. Every other figure is a silhouette with no face, number or name | A film shows what a role does, never who is who (the Insider's and Hacker's knowledge stay in their files) |
| **Foreshadowing, not an event.** The panel aboard glimpses the work ahead of the role on the Mothership; it shows no event of the match. No ability is used, no resource spent and no room assigned: the silhouettes are nobody, the places are the ship's and not a starting room (the Doctor's is the Hospital panel the Doctor's code opens), and nothing in the film is ever counted, logged or announced | Issue #188: the story may foreshadow the role's work aboard, but must not claim that a real ability, resource use or starting-room assignment occurred |
| **Every film ships to every phone:** the frame functions and art are one shared bundle, with every role's device and emblem and every crew character's portrait, and nothing is fetched for a dealt role | The art direction: "fetching only a dealt role or hidden action can disclose identity" |
| **Few words, and page text.** Only the two memory panels carry a narration box, a few words each, naming no role and stating no rule; the panel aboard and the title carry none (the owner: "it is like tutorial"). The narration is DOM text. The role's name in Ink Block lettering is decorative and is repeated in page text. Sound words are decoration and say nothing the picture does not | Captions translate and scale with the player's text size; a picture is never the only place a meaning appears |
| **Reduced motion:** three still pictures (the gadget raised in the briefing room, the full page with its two narration boxes, the title), cross-faded in 80 ms at 0, 3,000 and 7,800 ms. No travel, zoom, push, guided camera, panel focus, memory flash, glint, breathing, sway, rain, dust, impact lines, sheen, sparks or particles | The motion direction's reduced form |

## 2. The shared structure: a page from the ship's crew file

Every film is one page of the Mothership's classified crew file, told in a space-noir comic. The
ship is the city and the stars are the streetlights. The light falls through a viewport's slats as
it would through a detective's blinds. The film opens the night before the voyage, in the briefing
room at the dock, where the player takes up the role. As the gadget glints, the page turns to the
role-holder's past: a small back story, as a comic book gives its characters one.

| Beat | ms | Every role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opener: the briefing room** | 0–3,000 | Through a tall viewport the Mothership waits at its dock under the stars, the blinds half open, and the slats' light falls across a steel desk. The player's own role card stands on the desk in a steel holder, drawn as the phone draws it: their portrait, the role's held device, the role's name and the team line, straight and flat. A desk lamp behind it bends over it and pools its warm light on the card, and the role's gadget stands beside it. The player's own character leans in and reaches for the gadget (500–1,250 ms), takes it (1,300 ms) and raises it into the light, looking at it (1,400–2,250 ms), where it glints once (2,050–2,650 ms). The camera pushes in over the three seconds, the wall and viewport 2.5% and the character and desk 6.5%. This is the moment the player becomes the role |
| **The back story: three panels** | 3,000–7,800 | A comic tier of three panels slams in over the briefing room, which stays dimmed beneath: one at 3,000, one at 4,500 and one at 6,000. **Panels 1 and 2 are memory**: who the role-holder was before the voyage, in sepia. Each has a few words of narration in an aged-paper box riding its top edge, from 250 ms after it lands: the first until it fades as the third panel lands (5,900–6,100 ms), before the camera turns to it, and the second until the title. **Panel 3 is aboard, ahead**, in the night colours, at what the role does on the Mothership, without words. Each panel has a hard border, a gutter, and at most one comic sound word in Ink Block lettering |
| **Title** | 7,800–9,300 | The page darkens around a paper plate. The role's name stamps onto it in Ink Block lettering with a small overshoot. The team's emblem (Blue Planet, Red Planet or the Alien's spiral) sits in its corner, small |
| **Hold** | 9,300–10,000 | Everything settles. The page then gives way to the role card |

The words are few: a narration box of a few words on each memory panel, page text in the comic's
caption boxes. They are told to "you", so the back story fits whichever crew character the player is.
The back stories are fiction around the role: their words name no role and state no rule, and the
pictures show what the role does. The rules are the role card's. The opener, the panel aboard and
the title carry no captions. The role's name is on the card in the picture, and the page text
announces it at the title for screen readers.

**The look: an animated noir comic** (the owner's review of the pilot: "Animation comic book Noir AAA
style"). These are shared by every role and timed by the shared clock:
- **Depth.** Each panel is three layers (far, middle, near) under a slow push over its life, the near layer moving most. The opener is two layers. This is the motion comic's 2.5D camera.
- **Light.** Light falls in volumetric beams that are bright where they enter and gone where they land, with dust turning in them. Figures are navy silhouettes with a paper rim light and a pool of light on the wall behind. Steel takes bevel highlights. Screens and lamps glow and cast their light. Things stand on contact shadows.
- **Impact.** A panel lands with pale impact lines bursting from behind its edges, and they fade as it settles.
- **Memory.** The back story's two panels are drawn through one sepia colour matrix (`art.mjs` `SEPIA`) with a heavier vignette, and their narration boxes are aged paper.
- **The title.** After the stamp, a sheen crosses the plate once (8,320–8,920 ms), as light across a laminated file.
- **The memory flash** (2,650–3,300 ms). Warm light swells out of the gadget's glint, washes the page and clears on the back story's first panel. It is one slow swell, not a strobe, and the same for every role.
- **The guided view.** The camera eases onto each panel as it lands, over 450 ms with a light zoom (1.08) that keeps the panel's narration in view. While a later panel plays, the earlier ones step back in light. The camera pulls back to the whole page at 7,300–7,800 ms, before the title. The page text rides the camera: each narration box is returned where its panel is drawn. Zoomed in, a box is drawn in just far enough to stay whole in the frame, its stroke and shadow included. With the whole page in view, it overhangs its panel's edge a little, as before. No box is ever cut by the frame, and a box drops in only as it arrives, then leaves where it is.
- **Comic lettering.** The narration is set in capitals, a little tracked (CSS `text-transform: uppercase`; the words stay in sentence case for screen readers). It is broken as a letterer breaks a caption: a sentence to a line, and a sentence too long for one line split at its most even word gap (`captionLines()`), at most two lines of at most 17 characters. Each box is fitted to its lines at the phone's width with the camera pulled all the way back, so the words never wrap or spill (`narrationBox()`). Each narration box drops into place as it fades in.
- **Inking and print.** Figures in colour are inked with a bold outline. Panel edges are cross-hatched, and the whole page sits on a fixed paper grain.
- **Life.** Patients breathe under their sheets, and the colony ward's lamp sways on its cord. The player's character in the colony reads the chart and looks up.
- **Reduced motion** keeps the three stills, with none of the flash, camera, focus, breathing, sway, rain, dust, impact lines or sheen.
- **Review renders** are drawn at twice the phone's CSS pixels and encoded at CRF 18, so the review shows the ink and the lettering as a phone does.

**The gadget on the desk, by role.** The opener draws each role's own device, the one its role card
draws, without the hand, and without its team accent:

| Role | Gadget |
| --- | --- |
| Insider | the dossier tablet showing three faces |
| Doctor | the injector with its two vials |
| Blue Disabler and Red Disabler | the stun rod (the same rod; without its accent, the two are drawn alike) |
| Supplier | the twin supply canister |
| Engineer | the signal clamp |
| Undercover | the signal beacon |
| Hacker | the hacking box with its antenna |
| Alien | the crystal with its orbiting motes |

## 3. The storyboards: a back story for every role

Each film tells who the role-holder was before the voyage (panels 1 and 2, memory) and brings them
aboard (panel 3, a glimpse of the work ahead, never an event of the match). The back stories are fiction around the role, in the space-noir world of the
Mothership and its planets. The two memory panels carry a few words of narration each; panel 3 and
the title carry none. The pictures show what the rules give the role (the game design and the
owner's decisions), and the role card says it. The words are proposals; the game's words are
Frontend's. The narration is told to "you", so each back story fits whichever crew character the
player is. That character is the protagonist wherever a figure is shown in colour. Every film is
drawn (`design/cinematic/pilots/role-reveal/`, one module per film), each in the order and at the
times below.

### What the review found, and what changed

1. **The Engineer's blank card (0.5 s of `engineer-phone390-full.mp4`).** The card's portrait and device went missing in frames 15 and 16 (500 and 533 ms) and showed either side of them.
   - **The film itself was not at fault.** It placed both pictures identically in every frame from 433 to 600 ms.
   - **The cause was loading.** The stage drew each picture by reference to a decoded copy in a hidden SVG. When Chromium released that copy, the picture had to be decoded and rasterised again, and a frame could be captured before it was. Two such captures agree, so the renderer's settle check passed. A phone drawing the film live could hit the same race.
   - **The fix.** `revealSvg()` now takes `pictures`: each shipped picture's SVG text, read once. Given it, the frame draws every picture inline (`art.mjs` `inlinePicture()`), its viewBox mapped to the image's box, clipped as an image is, and its ids scoped. The frame refers to no file, so nothing is fetched or decoded while the film plays. The review stage passes `pictures`, and Frontend should too.
   - **Checks.** A test confirms that, with `pictures`, every frame of every role is free of `<image>` and of any external reference. The re-rendered Engineer and every other film were scanned frame by frame (section 6).
2. **The first caption clipped (6.5-7.5 s, 320×568 and 430×932).** On panel 3 the guided camera holds a 1.08 zoom, clamped to the page's foot. That takes the top 7.4% of the page out of the frame on every phone, 390×844 included, and the first box sits there.
   - **The fix.** The first box now fades out at 5,900-6,100 ms, as the third panel lands and before the camera turns to it. That comes after 2.65 s of reading; the second box stays until the title.
   - **Two edges found by the new test.** At the 1.08 zoom, each box's deliberate overhang past its panel's edge took its border about 5 px out of the frame on every phone. Zoomed in, a box is now drawn in just far enough to stay whole; with the whole page in view it overhangs as before. A box's drop-in no longer reverses on its way out: it fades where it is.
   - **Reduced motion is unchanged.** Both boxes stay on the page's still.
   - **Checks.** A test checks every frame of every film at seven phone sizes, in both variants: every box that shows at all lies wholly inside the frame.
3. **Exactly 10 s.** The review videos had 301 frames, 10.033 s, because the renderer also kept the end frame.
   - **The fix.** `cinematic-render.mjs --exact` encodes frames at i / 30 s for i < 300. The role films' renders use it: 300 frames, 10.000 s.
   - **Records.** The render records carry `frames: 300` and `encodedMs: 10000`, and the manifest's hashes were regenerated. The live clock is unchanged at 10,000 ms.

### Playback

- **Own phone only.** The film plays inside the existing role-reading stage, within its 30-second minimum. It never plays on the shared display or to anyone else, and the films are never played in sequence for one player.
- **Tap to skip.** A tap skips to the card. The card may offer "Watch again", which plays the same film.
- **No automatic replay.** A refresh or a reconnect during reading shows the card, not the film.
- **Backgrounding.** Going to the background (blur, or the page becoming hidden) stops the film and conceals the card, by the reviewed rule.
- **The film decides nothing.** Its end never confirms Ready, ends the reading stage, holds it open or advances the game. Ready stays the player's own tap.
- **Clock.** Local, from the film's first frame: it is private and keeps no shared time. The reading stage's deadline is the server's.
- **Not the boarding.** The films are separate from the shared boarding cinematic of issue #188, which plays after everyone is Ready.
- **Drawing each frame.**
  - Mount `svg`, then set the page text over the boxes in `words.story` (each with `lines`, `opacity` and `tone`); `revealWords()` gives the same page text without drawing. Set each box's lines one to a line, unwrapped (`white-space: pre`), in capitals with a little tracking (`text-transform: uppercase; letter-spacing: 0.04em`), at `captionFont(cssPx)` (13 px at least) inside `CAPTION_PAD`.
  - Announce `words.role` in a live region.
- **Silence.** No sound and no vibration.

### Limitations and dependencies

- **No phone has played them.** Every frame and video is desktop Chromium, and frame time on a phone is unmeasured. The films use SVG filters (the sepia matrix, the ink outline's `feMorphology`, blurs); if a frame runs long on a low-end phone, the outline is the first to drop.
- **Reading time.** The first box now reads for 2.65 s (3.25-5.9 s), the second for 3.05 s. Large text (200%) is Frontend's to fit, and reading time is Frontend's to try on a phone.
- **No contract change.** The reading stage, Ready and concealment already exist. Adoption is Frontend's and Integration's to schedule.
